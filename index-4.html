Strategic Use of Generative AI

Why Productivity ≠ Cost Benefit in GenAI

Use Case Categories Productivity vs Cost Use Case Cost Benefit Summary

Generative AI use cases can be strategically grouped into three categories—Defend, Extend, and Upend, each reflecting a different level of business transformation and value realization. These categories highlight whether GenAI is being used to protect existing operations, enhance current offerings, or create entirely new business models. Understanding the focus and financial impact of each category is essential for aligning AI initiatives with organizational goals. The table below outlines these categories, illustrating how productivity gains and financial benefits vary across different types of GenAI deployments.

2. Why Productivity Use Cases Don’t Equate to Cost Benefits

Productivity-focused use cases (common in Defend and some Extend scenarios) aim to save time, reduce manual effort, or improve efficiency. However, these do not always translate to cost benefits due to the following reasons:

1. Implementation and Ongoing Operation Costs

  • Model integration (APIs, prompt engineering, fine-tuning)
  • Infrastructure (cloud GPUs, pipelines)
  • Security, governance, compliance
  • Compute (API usage/GPU costs)
  • Updates & Monitoring

Even if a Gen AI tool makes employees more productive, the initial spend can dilute short-term financial ROI.

Productivity gains are offset if the run-rate costs of maintaining the solution are high—especially in “defend” use cases where the business isn’t growing.

2. Diminishing Returns

Once easy wins (like drafting or summarizing or document intelligence) are automated, the marginal productivity gains shrink. Human oversight still needed for nuanced tasks (e.g., legal reviews, complex customer support) caps savings.

Productivity curves flatten quickly, limiting the long-term cost impact unless paired with transformational use cases.

3. Indirect Financial Impact

Productivity benefits often reduce effort or time, not cost or revenue. For example:

  • Automating content creation reduces writer time but doesn’t guarantee more sales.
  • Gen AI-assisted code generation speeds up delivery but doesn’t ensure new features drive revenue.
  • Security, governance, compliance

Productivity ≠ profit unless tied to direct business KPIs like conversions or revenue growth. It remains an indirect benefit.

Comparison with Revenue-Focused Use Cases (Upend/Extend)

Gen AI use cases tied to new revenue streams, such as:

  • Launching personalized Gen AI driven customer experiences
  • Monetizing Gen AI features in SaaS offerings

have clearer and more direct ROI, since the value created is incremental to the business, not just cost avoidance.
